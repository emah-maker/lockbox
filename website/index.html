How it works

Four steps to a focused hour.

  1. 1

    Set your timer

    Swipe up or down on the touchscreen to dial in hours, minutes and seconds — anywhere from a few minutes to 9 hours.

  2. 2

    Drop the phone in

    Place your phone inside and close the opaque lid. Out of sight, out of reach, out of mind.

  3. 3

    The box locks

    The servo drives the latch shut and the countdown begins. The box will not open on its own until time is up.

  4. 4

    Unlock when done

    When the timer expires the latch releases automatically. In a real emergency, the press-count override lets you out early.

Straight answers

Questions you're already asking.

Those boxes are usually transparent, feel disposable, and either have no override or an override so weak (access holes, prying) that it defeats the purpose. Phone Box is opaque, rechargeable, has a real touchscreen interface, and gives you a controlled, tunable way out for genuine emergencies.

A software blocker is one settings toggle away from defeat, and the phone stays in your hand the whole time. Phone Box physically holds the phone until the timer ends — there's no toggle, because there's no software layer to switch off.

Precommitment beats willpower — that's the premise the entire category is built on, backed by behavioral research from Princeton, Harvard, Stanford and Yale. You decide once, up front, when you're thinking clearly; the box holds you to it when the urge hits.

Press the override button a set number of times (25 by default, adjustable from 10 to 100 in settings) and the latch releases early. It's deliberate enough that you won't do it on a whim, but always available when you truly need it — the sane middle path between kSafe's no-way-out and a cheap box's easy bypass.

An optional Bluetooth app is on the roadmap for live status, focus stats, clock sync, and an "important call" alert-through. It's optional and one-time — no subscription — and it will never auto-unlock the box. The physical override stays the only emergency exit.
